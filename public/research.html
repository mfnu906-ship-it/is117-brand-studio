PUREFORM / Research / Source recheck November 11, 2026

Research behind
the everyday tee.

How can PUREFORM present an everyday T-shirt so a visitor can understand the product, consider a simple outfit idea, and choose without an overwhelming shopping experience? These notes compare official retailer pages and connect observations to prototype choices.

This is desk research of public retailer pages, not customer interviews, a survey, or sales research. Observations, interpretations, and assumptions are labeled separately. Retailer pages can change.

01 / Question and method

What did I look for?

I reviewed official US T-shirt or tops pages on November 11, 2026. The source date makes it possible for another person to revisit the same pages.

I looked for how each retailer organizes basic T-shirts, presents fit or product choices, and helps a visitor imagine wearing the item. I recorded what appeared on the source page, then wrote my interpretation and proposed PUREFORM decision separately.

02 / Competitor research

Three source pages.

Open each original retailer page to inspect the evidence. These observations describe the pages, not what every customer thinks or buys.

UNIQLO — Men's T-Shirts ↗

Reviewed November 11, 2026

Evidence observed: The page describes T-shirts for everyday comfort, easy layering, and versatile wear. It separates short-sleeve and long-sleeve sections and offers sort choices such as price low-to-high and top rated.

Interpretation: This is a product-first way to organize basic T-shirts, with clear category structure and concise product descriptions.

PUREFORM decision: Keep one main shirt, product information, and size choices together instead of creating a large catalog.

This does not prove that PUREFORM customers want one style or that this layout increases sales.

H&M — Men's Basic T-Shirts ↗

Reviewed November 11, 2026

Evidence observed: The category shows regular, slim, relaxed, and oversized fits. Its description also mentions V-neck and crewneck styles, short and long sleeves, different colors, and multipack options.

Interpretation: H&M makes a broad range of product choices visible in one category. This offers variety but creates more options to compare.

PUREFORM decision: Keep the prototype to one example shirt and a short S, M, L, and XL demo size selector.

This does not establish which fit or size young adults prefer. The PUREFORM product and price data are fictional demo content.

Cotton On — Men's Tops ↗

Reviewed November 11, 2026

Evidence observed: The page includes “How to Style Men’s T-Shirts and Tanks.” It suggests combinations such as an essential tee with straight denim and sneakers, or a tee with a hoodie or overshirt, and gives study and casual examples.

Interpretation: This page adds everyday outfit context instead of only listing products.

PUREFORM decision: Add a short free styling tip and optional style goals so visitors can imagine an outfit without having to follow a suggestion.

This does not prove PUREFORM visitors need styling advice or prefer these outfit combinations.

03 / Compare alternatives

What should PUREFORM borrow?

The retailers use different approaches. I chose a focused mix for this first one-shirt prototype.

Product-first — UNIQLO

Clear T-shirt categories and concise product information. Useful for keeping the main product easy to inspect.

Many choices — H&M

Several fits, colors, sleeve types, and multipacks. Useful as a comparison, but broader than the current PUREFORM project scope.

Outfit context — Cotton On

Written styling combinations tied to study and casual situations. Useful for one small, optional styling suggestion.

Selected approach: one clearly presented T-shirt with a little optional styling help. This is a design decision based on the project's scope, not a proven customer preference.

04 / Positioning

Two possible positions.

The retailer pages show different product presentations; they do not prove which position will succeed for PUREFORM.

Affordable everyday basics

Simple T-shirts intended to feel accessible and easy to style. This fits the current PUREFORM brief and the friendly Everyman character.

Premium minimalist essentials

Minimalist T-shirts presented as more exclusive or higher priced. I did not choose this because I want PUREFORM to feel accessible rather than exclusive.

Chosen: affordable everyday basics

This is my brand-position decision. It is not a finding about what customers will pay. The $28 price shown in the prototype is fictional classroom-demo content, not market research.

05 / Research to features

From finding to check.

Each decision links a source observation to something visible in the prototype and a way to verify its behavior.

Focused product presentation

Finding: UNIQLO organizes its T-shirts by type and describes individual products.

Decision: PUREFORM shows one main T-shirt with a large image, short information, and size choices together.

Implementation: PUREFORM product section ↗

Check: Browser tests cover size selection, adding and emptying the demo bag, keyboard operation, and narrow-screen layout in the browser test file ↗.

Limited prototype choices

Finding: H&M shows many fits, colors, sleeve styles, and multipacks in one category.

Decision: Keep the current prototype to one example shirt and four demo size choices rather than building a full catalog.

Implementation: Size selector ↗

Check: Browser tests verify the size controls and that adding without a size displays an error and leaves the demo bag empty.

Optional styling ideas

Finding: Cotton On provides outfit examples that combine a T-shirt with denim, sneakers, or a layer.

Decision: PUREFORM adds a free styling tip and optional goals: “Keep my outfit simple” and “Match this with clothes I own.” Shopping does not require choosing a goal.

Implementation: Styling tip and style goals ↗

Check: Browser tests verify both matching suggestions and that shopping works with no goal selected. A reviewer retry is requested in open review Issue #9 ↗.

Automated checks verify the prototype's tested behavior. They do not prove the audience is validated, that a feature is useful to customers, or that the product claims are true.

06 / Audience hypotheses

Two possible people.

These are planning hypotheses, not validated customer research.

Persona A — College student

A college student aged 18–25 who attends classes, meets friends, and wants a simple T-shirt that can work with everyday outfits.

Possible concern: Will the shirt work for different casual situations and fit within a budget?

Persona B — Minimalist wardrobe builder

A young adult aged 18–25 who prefers a consistent, simple wardrobe and wants versatile basics that coordinate with clothes they already own.

Possible concern: Will the shirt work with outfits they already have?

Chosen audience

Young adults ages 18–25, especially college students. This is my working audience hypothesis. I have not validated it through interviews, a survey, or sales data.

07 / Brand and persuasion

Simple, friendly, honest.

Keep decisions distinct from claims and customer evidence.

Brand character

PUREFORM uses the Everyman archetype and a friendly, simple, approachable voice. It should feel relatable and useful for everyday people, not luxurious or intimidating.

Visual direction

Modernist: clean layout, clear hierarchy, simple typography, open spacing, neutral colors, and rust-red accents. This is intended to make product information easy to scan; it is a design rationale, not a measured usability result.

Honest persuasion

Do not invent reviews, limited stock, discounts, material details, sustainability claims, or product-performance claims. Styling suggestions are optional and do not block shopping.

08 / Peer review and open questions

What still needs checking.

The research record and issue links preserve feedback, decisions, changes, and reviewer follow-up.
